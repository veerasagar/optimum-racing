| 1 | Explainable Reinforcement Learning for Formula One Race Strategy | Devin Thomas, Junqi Jiang, Avinash Kori, Aaron Russo, Steffen Winkler, Stuart Sale, Joseph McMillan, Francesco Belardinelli, Antonio Rago | 2025 | This paper introduces RSRL, a Reinforcement Learning (RL) model, for optimizing Formula One race strategies, specifically tyre selection and pitstop timing. The work emphasizes explainability by integrating XAI techniques to enhance user trust and understanding of the model's decisions, offering a faster alternative to traditional Monte Carlo simulations. | The authors developed RSRL, a Deep Recurrent Q-Network (DRQN), trained using a Monte Carlo race simulator. The state space and reward function were carefully designed.  XAI techniques including TimeSHAP, VIPER and Decision Tree Counterfactuals were used to explain the model's decisions. | RSRL achieved a better average finishing position (P5.33) than both a Fixed Strategy (P5.63) and Mercedes' SOTA model (P5.86) in Bahrain GP simulations. Generalizability studies showed models trained on more tracks generalize better to unseen tracks. XAI methods demonstrated fidelity and comprehensibility, with examples showing alignment with real-world strategic thinking. | The evaluation is primarily within a simulated environment. While demonstrating improved performance and explainability, the model may not fully capture all complexities of real-world F1 racing and strategic decision-making beyond tyre strategy and pitstops. |

Minimum qualifying lap time across Q1, Q2, Q3 was used as input. | The model was evaluated by comparing predictions with actual 2023 race results. For most circuits, the deviation was controlled below 5%. However, some circuits showed higher deviations (e.g., Circuit Gilles Villeneuve - 16.9%, Silverstone Circuit - 12.1%, Circuit de Barcelona-Catalunya - 8.3%). For Albert Park Grand Prix Circuit deviation was 6.6%, Bahrain International Circuit 1.3%, Circuit de Monaco 1.9%, Hungaroring 0.5%, Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps 1.6%, Red Bull Ring 0.1%.  | The model, being a SISO system, has limitations in considering abstract and non-digital factors influencing lap times. Some predictions showed considerable deviations, indicating the model's struggle with unexpected changes or factors not captured in historical data.  |
| 3 | On the Optimization of Pit Stop Strategies via Dynamic Programming | Oscar F. Carrasco Heine, Charles Thraves | 2024 |  Presents and compares two dynamic programming models (deterministic and stochastic) for optimizing pit-stop strategies in motor racing, specifically Formula 1. The stochastic model incorporates uncertainty like weather and yellow flags to improve strategy decisions. | Develops a Deterministic Dynamic Programming (DP) and a Stochastic Dynamic Programming (SDP) formulation. These models consider factors like tire compound, wear, fuel, and random events to determine optimal pit stop laps and tire choices to minimize race time. |  Evaluates both DP and SDP models using numerical computations on a simulated 52-lap race.  Shows SDP outperforms DP, especially with frequent yellow flags, achieving lower race times on average. DP and SDP times are compared on 1000 sampled scenarios. For example, with soft tires and two yellow flags, SDP is ~0.22 seconds faster on average. |  Model simplifies real-world complexity by neglecting driver interactions and game theory aspects. Yellow flags are simplified as VSC events between laps start and end. The model's practical applicability in highly competitive real-world scenarios could be further investigated. |
| 4 | AI-enabled prediction of sim racing performance using telemetry data | Fazilat Hojaji, Adam J. Toth, John M. Joyce, Mark J. Campbell | 2024 | This paper explores the application of data science and machine learning (ML) in sim racing to identify key in-game metrics that predict performance. The study aims to identify metrics that predict performance across skill levels, investigate behavioral patterns, and determine track corner difficulty. | Telemetry data was collected from 174 participants using a professional simulator and MoTec i2 Pro while racing on the Brands-Hatch circuit in Assetto Corsa Competizione. Laps were clustered based on performance, and driving behaviors within performance groups were analyzed. Feature selection was performed using correlation analyses and ML models, with XGBoost chosen as the best classifier. | The best model achieved a prediction accuracy of 97.19%, indicating effective capture of critical performance factors. Average speed, lateral acceleration, steering angle, and lane deviation were identified as key metrics. The analysis also categorized corner difficulty and visualized driving patterns for FAST and SLOW laps, revealing differences in throttle, braking, and steering control. | Limitations include the MoTec data not capturing off-tracking, potential class imbalance in the dataset (more FAST laps than SLOW), and the study being limited to a single track. Future research may explore additional ML approaches, address class imbalance more robustly, and extend the analysis to multiple tracks. |
| 5 | Mastering Nordschleife - A comprehensive race simulation for AI strategy decision-making in motorsports | Max Boettinger, David Klotz | 2023 | This paper develops a novel race simulation model for GT racing, specifically on the Nordschleife, and integrates it with reinforcement learning (RL) to automate strategic decisions like pit stop timing. The study explores the potential of RL to optimize race strategy beyond Formula 1. | The authors developed a lap-wise race simulation model incorporating factors like fuel consumption, tire degradation, probabilistic events (accidents, safety cars), and multi-class traffic. They then trained a Deep Q-Network (DQN) agent within this simulation environment to make pit stop decisions. Historical data from the 2020 Nürburgring Langstrecken Serie was used for empirical parameter validation. | The RL agent, particularly with optimized hyperparameters, showed significant improvements over a baseline Q-Learning model. The trained agent made sensible pit stop decisions, mirroring real-world GT race strategies, such as adjusting refueling amounts and leveraging the splash-and-dash tactic in later stages. Convergence was observed around 70,000 episodes with consistent first-place finishes in simulations. | The paper acknowledges limitations such as the need for further research on probabilistic factors (GT-starts, multi-class traffic, topology-based parametrization) to enhance the simulation's robustness. The quality of the dataset was also a challenge for model parameterization. |
| 6 | Evolutionary F1 Race Strategy | Andrea Bonomi, Evelyn Turri, Giovanni Iacca | 2023 | This paper introduces a Genetic Algorithm (GA) to optimize Formula 1 race strategies by simulating various factors like weather, tire choice, pit-stops, and fuel weight. The GA aims to provide valuable insights for strategic decisions, offering a computationally efficient alternative to Monte Carlo simulations and Reinforcement Learning. | A custom GA was developed, where each individual represents a race strategy defined by tire compound, fuel load, and pit-stop decisions for each lap. The fitness function minimizes the total race time, considering time losses from tire compound, wear, fuel weight, pit-stops, and weather. The GA incorporates constraints like mandatory tire compound changes and fuel capacity. | Evaluated on both video-game simulations and real data from Pirelli for 2021 circuits (Bahrain, Barcelona, Monza, Portimao, Silverstone, Spielberg, Zandvoort). In simulations, GA performed close to brute-force, sometimes outperforming it in mixed weather. With real data, GA's strategies were comparable to or better than Pirelli's proposals, often finding faster strategies or similar ones with minor pit-window variations. Computation time was low, reaching a solution in under a minute for simulations and seconds for real data. | The model simplifies weather conditions and fuel consumption. Real-world factors like driver preferences and car-specific details are not explicitly incorporated, limiting strategy specificity. While computationally efficient, potential improvements include parallelization and more complex genotype representations. |
| 7 | Bayesian analysis of Formula One race results: disentangling driver skill and constructor advantage | Erik-Jan van Kesteren and Tom Bergkamp | 2023 | This paper introduces a novel Bayesian multilevel rank-ordered logit regression model to analyze Formula One race results from the hybrid era (2014-2021). The study aims to disentangle the relative contributions of driver skill and constructor advantage to race outcomes and rank drivers and constructors accordingly. | The authors collected race data from the Ergast motorsports API and Wikipedia, focusing on finished races and adding predictors like weather and circuit type. They developed a Bayesian multilevel rank-ordered logit model to analyze race finishing positions, using Stan software for estimation and Markov Chain Monte Carlo sampling for posterior inference. Model comparison was performed using leave-one-out cross-validation, and model checking was done via posterior predictive checks. | The model accurately represents race outcomes and disentangles driver skill and constructor advantage. Hamilton and Verstappen are identified as the best drivers, and Mercedes, Ferrari, and Red Bull as the top constructors in the hybrid era. Constructor advantage explains approximately 88% of the variance in race results. The model also allows for counterfactual predictions, such as estimating the win probability of Hamilton in an Alfa Romeo against Räikkönen in a Mercedes. | The model focuses on finished races, excluding non-finishers, which might omit information about driver reliability. The model's predictive capability is limited as it relies on past data and does not incorporate real-time race dynamics or external factors for forecasting future races. The yearly form parameters, while capturing seasonal variations, are unavailable for forecasting before a season starts. |
| 8 | Optimizing Pit Stop Strategies with Competition in a Zero-Sum Feedback Stackelberg Game in Formula 1 | Felipe Aguad, Charles Thraves | 2023 |  Presents a game theory model for optimizing pit stop strategies in Formula 1 races, considering competition between two drivers and uncertain events like yellow flags. Formulates the problem as a zero-sum feedback Stackelberg game solved using Dynamic Programming. |  Dynamic Programming to solve a zero-sum feedback Stackelberg game. Model incorporates stochastic elements like yellow flags and lap time uncertainty. Considers sequential decisions during the race and simultaneous initial tire choice.  | Numerical instances with millions of states are solved. Results show strategic decision making enhances winning odds (up to 15% increase against non-strategic opponent). Yellow flags favor drivers with worse performance. Example results:  Strategic players maximizing time gap win in 66.01% of simulated races. Standard deviation of final time gap is 2.00s in this case. |  Model focuses on two drivers. Extension to more drivers might increase computational complexity significantly.  |

The model utilizes embeddings to handle sparse input features and aims to assist team principals and engineers in making real-time pitstop decisions for better race outcomes. | Two EDNNs were developed: one to predict driver rank and another to predict the optimal pitstop lap. Historical data from 2015-2022 was used for training. Feature engineering and filtering were applied to the dataset. The model was trained using Python, Keras, and TensorFlow. | The model achieved a 93% correlation between predicted and actual driver rank. For pitstop prediction, precision was 0.56 and recall was 0.83, resulting in an F1 score of 0.67. RMSE for driver rank prediction was 2.51 (training) and 2.05 (test). R2 score was 0.42 (training) and 0.39 (test). | The model does not consider wet weather races and complex scenarios like dynamic opponent strategies or detailed car sensor data. Predictive accuracy under safety car phases could be improved. |

| 13 | Formula-E race strategy development using distributed policy gradient reinforcement learning | Xuze Liu, Abbas Fotouhi, Daniel J. It discusses various OR techniques like optimization (using Maple optimizer), Monte Carlo simulation, and discrete event simulation. The paper highlights the importance of logistics optimization in F1 for efficient transportation and resource management. | The paper points out limitations of different OR techniques in F1, such as the computational inefficiency and data intensiveness of Monte Carlo simulation, challenges in logistics standardization due to frequent circuit changes, inaccuracies in discrete event simulation due to real-world complexities, and limitations in front-wing optimization due to yearly regulation changes. | 18 | Application of Monte Carlo Methods to Consider Probabilistic Effects in a Race Simulation for Circuit Motorsport | Alexander Heilmeier, Michael Graf, Johannes Betz, and Markus Lienkamp | 2020 | This paper presents a method to enhance race simulations by incorporating probabilistic effects such as accidents, safety cars, driver start performance, and lap time variability using Monte Carlo Simulation (MCS) to improve race strategy robustness. | The authors extended a lap-wise discretized race simulation model to include probabilistic influences. They modeled starting performance, lap time variability, pit stop duration variability, accidents/failures, and full course yellow phases using customized probability distributions and a novel "ghost car" approach for safety cars. Empirical data from Formula 1 (2014-2019 seasons) was used to parameterize the models. MCS with 10,000 simulation runs was used to evaluate the interaction of these influences. | The results demonstrate the validity of the probabilistic models and the effectiveness of MCS in assessing race strategy robustness. The paper shows how MCS provides a distribution of race outcomes and rank positions, offering more comprehensive information for strategy engineers compared to deterministic simulations. The "ghost car" approach for safety car modeling accurately reflects the race dynamics. | The paper acknowledges some inaccuracies, such as lap-wise discretization limitations in modeling safety car behavior and the simplification of considering only one driver per accident. Data limitations also restrict detailed modeling of damaged cars and conditional probabilities for VSC phases after failures. The computational cost of MCS is also a consideration, although manageable with the proposed approach. |

| 20 | "Planning Formula One race strategies using discrete-event simulation" | J Bekker and W Lotz | 2009 | The paper presents a discrete-event simulation model for Formula One races.
